\section*{Background}
The Danish Emergency Management Agency recommends that every household in Denmark keeps
enough water, food and other essentials to manage on its own for at least three days.
That could be during a power outage, a failure in the water supply, severe weather or
problems with supply chains~\cite{brs-forberedt}. The guideline is 3 litres of water per
person per day and food that keeps without a fridge and needs no cooking~\cite{brs-forberedt}.
We expect that many households have not built such a stock, and we see these barriers:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item It is hard to work out \emph{what} to keep in stock and \emph{how much}.
  \item The stock has to match the number of adults and children in the household.
  \item The number of people changes over time, and the stock has to change with it.
  \item Expiry dates pass, and the stock gets old without anyone noticing.
  \item It is a hassle to remember to reorder by hand.
\end{itemize}
Online grocery shops deliver food to the door, but the customer still has to decide
what to buy and keep an eye on the expiry dates. Delivery alone does not remove any of
the barriers above.

\section*{Problem Statement}
How can a subscription service let a customer get an emergency stock that follows the
Danish Emergency Management Agency's three-day guideline, and keep it in date without
the customer having to track expiry dates or remember to reorder?

We assume that the main barrier is the effort of maintaining the stock, not the cost, and
that a fixed package per adult and per child is precise enough to meet the guideline.

\section*{Project Description}
In the project we develop PrepMeUp. It is a subscription service that sends the customer
an emergency stock made of packages and replaces it before it expires. The grocery
supplier and the payment are simulated in the project.

\subsection*{System Components}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item In the customer app, the customer signs up, composes and changes orders, and sees the subscription status and notifications.
  \item In the administrator interface, the administrator maintains the package types and sees and sends upcoming orders.
  \item The PrepMeUp system stores customers, subscriptions, orders and package types. It plans the next order before the previous one expires and notifies customers and administrators.
  \item The supplier and the payment are simulated with a product catalogue and a fake card payment. No real supplier or payment provider is connected.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Core Flow}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item The customer creates an account and signs up for a subscription.
  \item The customer composes the order from package types. The customer chooses how many adult and child packages it contains and of which type, for example \emph{Vegetarian -- Adult}. Equipment such as a flashlight is its own package type and can be added too.
  \item The administrator sends the order, and the system records the order's expiry date.
  \item Before the order expires, the system plans the next order as a copy of the previous one and notifies the customer.
  \item While the next order is planned, the customer can change it, or pause or cancel the subscription.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Scope}
The system covers food, water and simple equipment such as a flashlight. There is no
real grocery supplier or payment provider. Both are simulated. Medicine, other
prescription or regulated goods, and packages made for allergies are out of scope.
