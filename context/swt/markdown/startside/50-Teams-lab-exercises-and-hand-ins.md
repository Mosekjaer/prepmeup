# Teams, lab exercises and hand-ins in I4SWT

<!-- side 1 -->

A note on teams, lab exercises and hand-ins in I4SWT. Version January 2023.

1    Introduction
This note presents some general guidelines on teams, lab exercises and hand-ins in the course “Software Test”
(SW4SWT).

2    Teams and lab exercises
Software development is teamwork, and testing is an integral part of software development. Therefore, software test-
ing is done in a team. To train this, your use of version control systems and continuous integration in a team setting is
a cornerstone of the course.

Many lab exercises as well as the mandatory hand-ins are, with no exceptions, completed in teams. The requirements
for the teams and their submissions are as follows:

    •    Each team shall consist of 3-4 students. No more, no less1.
    •    Each student shall state his/her name, student number and team number on all material that is submitted in
         the course (see below).
    •    You shall form the teams yourselves.
    •    Each team member shall sign up to a group on Brightspace

You are free to split, join, merge, shuffle etc. teams as long as the above requirements are fulfilled and the teachers
are notified. You can leave and join groups yourself on Brightspace. Group self-enrollment ends before the handin of
mandatory exercise number 1, after that you must contact a teacher to be enrolled in a group.

3    Hand-ins
The intention of the hand-ins is to allow you to train your understanding of the topics covered in each hand-in, and to
train you in the presentation of your results. All hand-ins in this course are mandatory. The details are laid out below.

    •    If you do not take part in all 3 mandatory hand-ins, you will not be allowed to take part in the final exam
    •    The hand-ins do not count toward your grade for the course
    •    Since they are mandatory, your name must be on the front page of the hand-in paper, together with the other
         members of the team. This indicates that you have taken part in the work that the paper describes.
    •    The deadline of the hand-in must be respected, otherwise it will not count as fulfilling the mandatory hand-in
    •    The hand-in is evaluated for acceptability according to a set of criteria given (in Rubrics)
    •    If the evaluation shows a hand-in that is not acceptable, there are 2 possibilities:
              o The team is given the opportunity to submit a new version before a given time and date
              o The team is not given the opportunity to submit a new version, and participation in the final exam is
                   therefore not possible. This will especially be the outcome, if it is obvious to the teacher that the team
                   has not put in a serious effort to solve the hand-in

4    Hand-ins and the final oral exam
The actual content of the group hand-in problems and your solutions are not the direct topic of the examination.

The exam will be based on a single-person hand-in, with theoretical and practical questions, to be finished within a 24
hour period after the publication. It will be followed by a 30 minute exam on one of the following days, where the
exam hand-in will form the basis of the examination.

But you are free to refer to your experience and specific solution details from the group hand-ins, to explain or relate
to the topics covered in the course that will be the direct examination topics. In fact, we recommend that you do this.

If needed, we may ask you during the examination about your group hand-ins, to help you getting started on a topic.
This will not be a check on your hand-in participation, only a helping hand to make it possible for you to show your
understanding of the topics, if the examination is not going in that direction.

1
 Under very special circumstances shall groups of two students be allowed. This is only permitted with the expressed
prior consent of the teachers of the course. Under no circumstances shall solo work (“group of 1”) be permitted, so
you need not ask!
