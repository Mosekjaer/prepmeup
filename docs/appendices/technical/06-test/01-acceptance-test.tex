\section{Test Environment}
The tests are run against the deployed system, from a device outside the server. The customer app is installed on an Android device from the latest EAS build, and the administrator interface is opened in a browser.

Customer A and customer B register through the customer app (FR-01) and get the household role. Administrator ADM is a user who has been given the admin role in the Keycloak admin console.

Some tests call the API directly, and they need an access token. The clients do not let you log in with a password from the command line. Instead the tester logs in through the administrator interface, opens the browser's developer tools and copies the Authorization header from a request to the API. A token is valid for 15 minutes.

Tests that need orders with given dates use test data that is put into the database before the test. The data is described in the precondition of each test, and all dates are relative to the day the test is run.

NFR-03, NFR-04 and NFR-07 stop or start a single container, so the tester needs access to the server.

\section{Functional Requirements}
This section tests the must-have functional requirements in appendix 1.2, which are FR-01--06 and FR-19. Each test is a scenario that is run step by step against the deployed system. The actual result and the verdict are filled in when the test is run. The should-have and could-have requirements get tests when they are planned into a sprint.

\subsection{FR-01: Create account and subscription}
\scenarioinfo{FR-01}
  {Main scenario: A new customer creates an account, completes onboarding and signs up for a subscription.}
  {The customer app is installed on an Android device. The tester has an e-mail address that is not registered yet.}
  {The customer has an account, name and address are stored, and the customer has an active subscription.}
\begin{steps}
1 & The customer opens the app and taps Sign in. & The PrepMeUp login page of the identity provider opens, with a link to register. & & \\
\hline
2 & The customer registers with the e-mail address and a password. & The app opens again and shows the onboarding screen, which asks for name and address. & & \\
\hline
3 & The customer saves onboarding with an empty name. & The details are not saved, and a message says that the name is missing. & & \\
\hline
4 & The customer enters name and address and saves. & The app shows the home screen with the customer's name. & & \\
\hline
5 & The customer chooses to sign up for a subscription. & The app shows the subscription with status active. & & \\
\hline
6 & The customer closes the app, opens it again and signs in. & The app does not show onboarding again. The subscription is still active, and name and address are the ones from step 4. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}

\subsection{FR-02: Persistent storage}
\scenarioinfo{FR-02}
  {Main scenario: Customers, subscriptions and orders are kept when the back-end restarts.}
  {Customer A has completed onboarding and has an active subscription, one sent order and one planned order (test data).}
  {The data is unchanged after the restart.}
\begin{steps}
1 & The tester signs in as A and writes down name, address, subscription status and, for each order, its status and packages. & The values are written in the actual result column. & & \\
\hline
2 & The tester restarts the API and the database containers without removing their volumes. & Both containers are running again within 2 minutes. & & \\
\hline
3 & The tester signs in as A again. & Name, address, subscription status and both orders are the same as in step 1. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}

\subsection{FR-03: Default package types}
\scenarioinfo{FR-03}
  {Main scenario: The default package types for a normal diet exist without an administrator creating them.}
  {The system has just been deployed with an empty database. No administrator has created a package type.}
  {None.}
\begin{steps}
1 & The tester signs in as ADM and opens the list of package types (or requests it through the API). & Exactly one package type for adults and one for children are listed, both for a normal diet. & & \\
\hline
2 & The tester opens each of the two package types. & Each package type contains at least one item with a quantity greater than 0. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}

\subsection{FR-04: Expiry date of orders}
\scenarioinfo{FR-04}
  {Main scenario: The system registers an expiry date for each order when it is sent.}
  {Customer A has two planned orders, O1 and O2 (test data). ADM is signed in.}
  {Each sent order has its own expiry date.}
\begin{steps}
1 & ADM marks O1 as sent (FR-06). & O1 is registered as sent with today's date. & & \\
\hline
2 & The tester looks up O1 through the API with ADM's token. & O1 has a sent date and an expiry date that is later than the sent date. & & \\
\hline
3 & ADM marks O2 as sent, and the tester looks up both orders again. & O2 has its own expiry date. The expiry date of O1 is unchanged. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}

\subsection{FR-05: See the next orders to send}
\scenarioinfo{FR-05}
  {Main scenario: The administrator sees the planned orders, sorted by planned send date, earliest first.}
  {ADM is signed in. Test data: planned orders for customers A (send date today+9), B (today+2) and C (today+5), and one sent order for customer D.}
  {None.}
\begin{steps}
1 & ADM opens the list of orders to send. & The list shows exactly three orders, in the order B, C, A. The order for D is not shown. & & \\
\hline
2 & ADM marks B's order as sent and reloads the list. & The list shows C, then A. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}

\subsection{FR-06: Register an order as sent}
\scenarioinfo{FR-06}
  {Main scenario: The administrator marks an order as sent.}
  {ADM is signed in. A planned order for customer A is shown in the list of orders to send (FR-05).}
  {The order has a sent date and is no longer planned.}
\begin{steps}
1 & ADM marks the order as sent. & The order disappears from the list of orders to send. & & \\
\hline
2 & ADM reloads the page. & The order is still gone from the list. & & \\
\hline
3 & The tester looks up the order through the API with ADM's token. & The order has today's date as sent date. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}

\subsection{FR-19: Next order 14 days before expiry}
\scenarioinfo{FR-19}
  {Main scenario: The system creates the next order for a customer with an active subscription 14 days before the previous order expires.}
  {Test data: customers C15, C14 and CP each have one sent order with 2 adult packages and 1 child package, and no planned order. The order of C15 expires today+15, the orders of C14 and CP expire today+14. C15 and C14 have an active subscription, CP has a paused subscription. ADM is signed in.}
  {C14 has exactly one new planned order. C15 and CP have none.}
\begin{steps}
1 & The tester waits until the back-end has checked the expiry dates, and ADM opens the list of orders to send. & The list contains a planned order for C14 and none for C15 or CP. & & \\
\hline
2 & ADM opens the new order for C14. & The order contains 2 adult packages and 1 child package of the same package types as the previous order. & & \\
\hline
3 & The tester waits until the back-end has checked the expiry dates again, and ADM reloads the list. & C14 still has exactly one planned order. & & \\
\hline
\end{steps}
