\section*{Background}
The Danish Emergency Management Agency recommends that every Danish household keep a
supply of water, food and other essentials sufficient to cover the household for at
least three days during emergencies such as power outages, water supply failures,
severe weather or disruptions to supply chains~\cite{brs-forberedt}. The agency's
guideline is 3 litres of water per person per day and food that keeps without
refrigeration and needs no cooking~\cite{brs-forberedt}. We expect that many households
have not built such a stock, and we see these barriers:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item It is hard to work out \emph{what} and \emph{how much} one should keep in stock.
  \item The stock has to match the number of adults and children one prepares for.
  \item The number of people changes over time, and the stock has to change with it.
  \item Expiry dates quietly pass, and the stock becomes outdated without anyone noticing.
  \item Having to remember to reorder manually is inconvenient.
\end{itemize}
Online grocery shops deliver food to the door, but they leave it to the customer to decide
what to buy and to watch the expiry dates. None of the barriers above is solved by
delivery alone.

\section*{Problem Statement}
How can a subscription service let a customer get an emergency stock that follows the
Danish Emergency Management Agency's three-day guideline, and keep it in date without
the customer having to track expiry dates or remember to reorder?

We assume that the main barrier is the effort of maintaining the stock, not the cost, and
that a fixed package per adult and per child is precise enough to meet the guideline.

\section*{Project Description}
The project develops PrepMeUp, a subscription service that sends a customer an
emergency stock made of packages and replaces it before it expires. The grocery supplier
and the payment are simulated within the project.

\subsection*{System Components}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Customer app}: sign-up, composing and changing orders, subscription status and notifications.
  \item \textbf{Administrator interface}: maintain package types, and see and send upcoming orders.
  \item \textbf{PrepMeUp system}: stores customers, subscriptions, orders and package types, plans the next order before the previous one expires, and notifies customers and administrators.
  \item \textbf{Simulated supplier and payment}: a product catalogue and a mock card payment. No real supplier or payment provider is integrated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Core Flow}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item The customer creates an account and signs up for a subscription.
  \item The customer composes the order from package types, choosing how many adult and child packages it contains and of which type, e.g. \emph{Vegetarian -- Adult}. Equipment such as a flashlight can be added as its own package type.
  \item The administrator sends the order, and the system records the order's expiry date.
  \item Before the order expires, the system plans the next order as a copy of the previous one and notifies the customer.
  \item While the next order is planned, the customer can change it, or pause or cancel the subscription.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Scope}
The system covers food, water and simple equipment such as a flashlight. The system does
not integrate with an actual grocery supplier or payment provider; both are simulated.
Medicine and other prescription or regulated goods, and packages tailored to allergies,
are out of scope.
